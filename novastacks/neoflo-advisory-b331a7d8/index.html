From referrals to a repeatable pipeline

A short, structured read-out of our conversation. It's based only on what we discussed, with none of Neoflo's data reviewed, so treat it as hypotheses to check against your pipeline rather than conclusions.

The product isn't the problem. The system and the message are.

You've won 8–9 well-known customers through referrals and roundtables, which proves the product. What's missing is a pipeline that runs without you, a pitch in the buyer's language, and funnel numbers by ICP tier so channel and hiring decisions are made on data, not instinct.

01What we heard
Pipeline

Warm-only

Almost all deals come from referrals. Roundtables are the only channel that has reliably produced leads. There's no inbound or outbound motion yet.

Process

Person-driven

Deals depend on individual follow-up, not a system, so some fall through the cracks between SOW and pilot.

Buyers

Silent no's

Ghosting keeps happening. Finance buyers in particular rarely say no outright, so the real objections stay hidden.

Message

Strong product, weak landing

The product is good, but the pitch may not match how buyers want to hear it. You said yourself: "that might be the problem with us."

02Recommendations, in order

  1. Put the pipeline on rails Next 30 days

    Pull the full year of pipeline data into one place before spending on anything new. Then set three rules: every open deal has a named next step and a date; there's a set follow-up cadence after each meeting; and SOW → pilot → paid conversion is tracked as its own stage.

  2. Turn ghosting into data Next 30 days

    Ask every closed or stalled deal a short 3-question survey, or book a 10-minute call. For finance buyers, make "no" easy to say:

    • Offer multiple-choice answers ("Is it timing, budget, priority or fit?") instead of open questions.
    • Send a polite break-up email after two unanswered follow-ups. It often brings out the real reason.

  3. Rewrite the pitch in the buyer's words Days 30–60

    Use what the surveys turn up, plus quotes from your current customers, to rebuild the core message. For CFOs, lead with financial outcomes and risk reduction before features. Test the new version on the next 10 conversations.

  4. Reverse-engineer the funnel by ICP tier Days 30–60

    Start from your revenue target and work backwards. For each tier, estimate conversion rates, how many SQLs you need, and acquisition cost. Different tiers need different motions:

    SMBSmaller brands, ~$3K/monthSearch, content, self-serve or low-touch demoCAC vs. ~$3K MRR payback; volume needed for the tier to matter

  5. Test channels in 3-month sprints before committing budget Days 60–90+

    Scale what's proven first: build roundtables into a regular series with a set follow-up process. Then run one new channel at a time for 3 months, with a target set in advance (meetings, SQLs, cost per SQL). Keep it, change it or kill it based on the numbers.

03Team, hiring & the product idea
Brand consultant

Scope her to positioning

Brand work alone won't produce SQLs. Give her a fixed set of foundational deliverables with a deadline (ICP definition, positioning, a messaging house), and make sure someone has the capacity to put them into practice.

Growth hire

Build the system first

A full-time, numbers-driven, AI-fluent growth marketer is the right profile. Write a 90-day scorecard before you hire, source through referrals, and have the pipeline basics in place so they inherit a system to run, not chaos to untangle.

Marketing analytics

Validate before building

Two customers asking is a good signal. But the CFO and the marketing team are different buyers with different budgets. Interview both customers about who would own it and who would pay before putting it on the roadmap.
