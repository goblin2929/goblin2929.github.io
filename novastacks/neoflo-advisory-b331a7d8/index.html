Growth & go-to-market · one-page advisory

Double down on what works, and break the rest into workstreams

A short read-out of our conversation, written so we can use our next conversation on decisions rather than background. It's based only on what we discussed, without looking at Neoflo's data, services or product. The details will change once we do; the direction probably won't.

What you need sits between strategy and execution: about 20% strategy, 80% overseeing the work and feeding back on it.

A traditional full-time CMO is the wrong fit for that. Instead, set the sales & marketing budget at 20–25% of revenue, quarter by quarter, put most of the program spend behind roundtables, add two workstreams that run on time more than money (nurture, and founder-led partnerships), and divide the work: you take the senior bets, an advisor oversees the growth engines, and a mid-level team executes.

01What we heard
Pipeline

Warm-only

Almost all deals come from referrals. Roundtables are the only channel that has reliably produced leads. There's no inbound or outbound motion yet.

Process

Person-driven

Deals depend on individual follow-up, not a system, so some fall through the cracks between SOW and pilot.

Buyers

Silent no's

Ghosting keeps happening. Finance buyers in particular rarely say no outright, so the real objections stay hidden.

Message

Strong product, weak landing

The product is good, but the pitch may not match how buyers want to hear it. You said yourself: "that might be the problem with us."

20–25% of revenueon sales & marketing, people included: the norm for a venture-backed company aiming to grow

Build a pro forma plan that doubles revenue next year, and size the budget from it. Then release it each quarter against actual revenue, so you neither over-invest ahead of growth nor starve it. You don't have to spend all of it.

03Two workstreams that run on time more than money
Nurture · ~3–5% of the marketing budget

Regular, relevant updates to every MQL and key partner

Once someone becomes a lead, keep in touch by email on product progress, new developments and customer outcomes. Spend only a little on the tooling and content: ~3–5% of the marketing budget, since your current sales activity already covers part of it. This is product-led work, so keep it in-house with a product marketer who also understands how a marketing team runs, rather than a vendor. It's what keeps a 7–9 month pipeline from going cold.

Founder-led · new channels

Your network, partners and integrations

Open new channels through your own network: technology providers, industry bodies and community partners. Measure it by first meetings booked. Then the senior questions: partner and licensing programmes with AI platforms such as Anthropic, and whether an MCP integration makes sense for a service-led product. This is CEO-level integration strategy.

04People: divide and conquer

If you can find a senior director who works at roughly 20% strategy and 80% hands-on oversight, hire them. If not, split the role:

Nipun · senior bets

Integrations, partnerships, growth loop

Founder-network channels, AI-platform partnerships, the MCP and integration strategy, and the sales growth loop. You also decide which tests to fund, renew or kill.

Marketing advisor · Tina

Oversee the growth engines

Sets up and oversees the roundtable, AEO, outbound and nurture engines: frameworks, test design, funnel math by ICP tier, and regular feedback. Helps scope and screen the team.

Mid-level team · execution

AI-forward operators

An AI-forward growth marketer (roundtables, outbound, content) and a product marketer (nurture, messaging). Numbers-driven, comfortable building structure, fluent at putting AI tools to work. Source through referrals.
