Neoflo needs a growth system, not a full-time CMO

Based only on our conversation. We haven't reviewed Neoflo's data, services or product, so the figures are indicative; we expect the direction to hold.

Executive summary

Neoflo has proved its product. Eight to nine well-known e-commerce and retail brands pay for it, and they were won almost entirely through referrals and founder-hosted roundtables. That same pattern is now the constraint. Growth depends on your personal network and follow-up, the sales cycle runs seven to nine months, and buyers who lose interest go silent rather than saying no. Doubling revenue on this model would mean doubling your own time, which isn't possible. We recommend three moves.

  1. Tie the budget to the growth plan. Invest 20–25% of revenue in sales and marketing, sized from a plan that doubles revenue and released quarter by quarter against actual results.
  2. Concentrate spend on what already works, and cover the whole sales cycle. Put most of the money behind roundtables and the content they generate, scale AI visibility, and treat outbound as a contained experiment. Then add a nurture programme so deals stay warm until they close.
  3. Split the senior marketing role instead of hiring it. The work is about 20% strategy and 80% oversight and execution. You keep the senior bets, an advisor runs the growth engines, and a mid-level, AI-forward team does the work.

To move forward, we need three decisions from you: approve the plan to double and the budget rule, commit the first-quarter budget, and name an internal owner for messaging.

1Growth is proven but not yet repeatable

Neoflo wins large, credible customers when the right buyer meets the right person, and roundtables are the one channel that has reliably created those meetings. What's missing is a system around that success. There is no inbound or outbound motion, and the pipeline is managed through individual effort rather than process, so some deals fall away between statement of work and pilot.

Two further signals point to the same gap. Finance buyers rarely decline outright; they stop responding, so Neoflo loses deals without learning why. And by your own assessment, the pitch may not match how buyers want to hear it. Spending more on acquisition before fixing these would mostly speed up the leaks. The recommendations below are ordered to build the system first, then put money behind it.

2The budget should follow the growth plan, released quarter by quarter

A fixed annual marketing number either runs ahead of revenue or holds growth back. A better rule for a venture-backed company aiming to double is to invest 20–25% of revenue in sales and marketing, people included, sized from a pro forma plan and released each quarter against what revenue actually does. The budget is a ceiling, not a target. Neoflo should spend it only where a channel is earning its place.

As an illustration: at today's roughly $150–200K of revenue a quarter, the rule supports $30–50K of quarterly spend. On a path to about $1.4M next year, it supports roughly $280–350K for the year. Around $150K of that would fund marketing programmes and the rest the team.

3Most of the programme budget should back what already works

We suggest splitting programme spend 70 / 20 / 10 across proven channels, channels with early signal, and experiments. This puts most of the money where there is evidence, while protecting a small budget for learning that isn't expected to pay back in the first quarter.

Roundtables are the core. Running them every two weeks, at about $3K each, builds momentum without outpacing Neoflo's ability to follow up. Weekly would double the cost and risk the wrong audience. Each table should be invited from the target customer profile, followed up within 48 hours, and tracked to the deals it produces. The content that comes out of them, around $2K a month for social and written content, is a necessary cost rather than an optional one. A vendor or an in-house team can produce it, but a strong messaging owner inside Neoflo must direct it.

AI visibility is the channel to scale. Finance and e-commerce leaders increasingly research vendors through ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google's AI answers, and referred prospects check Neoflo there too. About $5K a month for six months makes sure buyers find the positioning your brand work is building, with a clear point to review.

Outbound is the experiment. It is the one channel where leads can be counted directly, which makes it a good test of whether Neoflo can create demand beyond its network. It should be judged on whether it shows a signal, not on early return. Fund it from marketing while it is a test and move it to the sales budget once it reaches an agreed meetings target. Either way it is an acquisition cost, not cost of goods sold.

4Marketing has to carry deals through a seven-to-nine-month cycle

Roundtables, outbound and AI visibility all create first meetings. With a sales cycle this long, that is where the risk begins, not where marketing's job ends. Neoflo needs a nurture programme: regular, relevant email updates to every qualified lead and key partner on product progress, new capabilities and customer results. Because this content comes from the product, it should be owned in-house by a product marketer who understands how a marketing function runs, rather than handed to a vendor. It needs only around 3–5% of marketing spend, because sales activity already covers part of it.

Alongside this, you are best placed to open founder-led channels that no hire could: introductions through technology providers, industry bodies and community partners, measured by first meetings booked. The more strategic questions also sit with you, including partner and licensing programmes with AI platforms such as Anthropic and whether an MCP integration makes sense for a service-led product.

5Split the senior marketing role rather than hire it full-time

What Neoflo needs sits between strategy and execution. A traditional full-time CMO would be paid mainly for strategy, typically works at a different pace from an early-stage company, and is often not fluent in the AI-assisted way this work is now done. If you can find a senior director who works hands-on, about 20% strategy and 80% oversight, hire them. Otherwise, divide the role three ways.

You keep the senior bets: integrations, ecosystem partnerships, founder channels and the sales growth loop, plus the final decision on which tests to fund, renew or stop. A marketing advisor sets up and oversees the growth engines (roundtables, AI visibility, outbound and nurture), designs the tests and funnel math, and gives regular feedback. A mid-level, AI-forward team does the work: a growth marketer for roundtables, outbound and content, and a product marketer for messaging and nurture. Both should be numbers-driven and are best found through referrals. The brand consultant's work should be limited to foundational positioning, which the team can then amplify.
