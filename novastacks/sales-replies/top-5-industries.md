# Lead reply — "What are the top 5 industries you primarily focus on?"

**Context:** cold one-liner from an inbound/outbound lead. Goal is not to list verticals —
it's to answer fast, prove depth with names, and hand the question back so they tell us
their category. Keep it under 120 words. Never open with "great question".

**Live lead: D2C skincare.** Use the versions in
[Section D](#d-d2c-skincare-version-live-lead) — they name his category, lead with the
founder-operator D2C credential, and close on a check he can run himself in 60 seconds.
Sections A–C stay as the generic fallback for the next lead.

brand's own site. So the reply does three things: put his category *inside* the five, prove
we've operated D2C as owners rather than as an agency, and end on a check he can run himself
before he replies.

### D3. The FOMO, and how to make it land

Four levers, in the order they actually work on a founder. Use one or two — stacking all
four reads as a pitch and kills the credibility the free audit is meant to buy.

**1. The 60-second self-check (curiosity — strongest, use in almost every send).**
Don't tell him he has a problem; hand him the query that shows it. *"Ask ChatGPT 'best
vitamin C serum for sensitive skin' and see who comes up."* He will run it. If a competitor
is named and he isn't, the FOMO is his own conclusion, which is the only kind that sticks.
This also costs us nothing and can't be wrong — whatever it returns is a conversation.

**2. Loss that's already happening (not a future risk).**
- AI-referred visitors convert **42% more often** than other traffic (Adobe) — for a D2C
  founder this is the number, because it's revenue per session, not impressions.
- **Two-thirds of searches now end with no click** (SparkToro) — the top-of-funnel traffic
  he used to buy is being answered before it reaches him.
- **85% of brand mentions** AI cites sit on third-party pages, and **60% of AI Overview
  citations** come from URLs outside the top 20 organic results (Brainlabs). Translation for
  skincare: his PDPs and his agency's blog are not what's being read. Reddit is.

**3. Compounding lock-in (why "later" is more expensive than "now").**
The echo layer hardens. Every new "best serums of 2026" roundup is written from the last
one, and models lean on what's already cited — so the brands named today become the default
named next quarter. Entering the answer set is cheap while it's still unsettled in a
category; displacing an incumbent costs multiples of that. Phrase as a fact about how the
mechanism works, never as a deadline we invented.

**4. Category exclusivity (real constraint — only if we're prepared to honour it).**
We can't run AEO for two brands competing for the same answer set; winning the "best serum
for X" prompt for one client means taking it from the other. So a category slot genuinely is
one-per-competitor-set. This is legitimate scarcity because it's structurally true. Say it
plainly, once: *"Worth flagging — we only take one brand per competitive set, since the whole
job is winning the same prompts."* Then drop it.

**The actionable half.** FOMO without a next step is just anxiety. Every version above ends
on the free prompt audit — his brand plus three named competitors, run through real buyer
prompts, results in a day. It's the standing offer on the deck ("See what AI says about your
brand today — we'll run the first audit free"), it requires nothing from him, and it converts
the fear into a scheduled deliverable. Ask for the three competitor names in the same
message: naming them is a micro-commitment and it tells us the set before the call.

**Speed proof, if he asks how long this takes.** GoFreight went from invisible to top-5
share of mentions in AI answers in **three months** — organic clicks +88%, average position
25 → 8. Different category, same method. Use it as evidence the window is short enough to
matter and short enough to act on, not as a promise of the same numbers.

### D4. Do not use

- Invented scarcity — "two slots left", "pricing goes up Friday", "onboarding closes this
  month". We haven't got a real constraint of that kind, and a skincare founder has seen the
  playbook a hundred times from performance agencies.
- Fabricated skincare client names or made-up category stats. Everything cited above is in
  the decks with a source. If he asks for a skincare case specifically, the honest answer is
  the strong one: *"Not skincare yet — which is exactly why the category is still winnable.
  What we have is $10M of our own D2C exits and a freight SaaS we moved in 90 days."*
- Leading with the AI-transformation or fractional-CMO services. He asked one question. Sell
  the audit, nothing else.

## Notes for whoever sends this

- **Adjacent verticals we've delivered in** but wouldn't lead with: telco (Singtel), proptech,
  events/expo, education. Mention them only if the lead is in one — telco especially is real,
  substantial work, it just doesn't earn a slot when the list is capped at five.
- **Pick the version by channel.** A one-line question deserves a short answer — version B
  or A. Version C only when they've already shown intent.
- **Always end on the question.** The list is the price of admission; their category is the
  thing we actually need to move the conversation.
- **If they're in none of the five:** "Not a vertical we've gone deep in — which usually
  means less competition for the AI answer, not more risk. The method doesn't change." Then
  ask what their buyers search before they buy.
- **Reorder the five to put the lead's category second**, never first. Second reads as an
  honest list that happens to include them; first reads as a list rewritten for the email.
- **Adapting D to another vertical:** the shape holds — name their category inside the five,
  attach the one credential that proves we've operated *in their world* rather than sold to
  it, hand them a query they can run themselves, close on the free audit. Only the proof
  point and the sample prompt change.
- Figures cited in versions C and D come from the credentials deck
  (`novastacks/company-credential/`) and the AEO deck (`novastacks/aeo-deck/`) — keep them in
  sync if either deck changes. Sources: Adobe Analytics (42% conversion lift), SparkToro
  (two-thirds zero-click), Brainlabs (85% third-party mentions, 60% outside top-20),
  client analytics (GoFreight).
