07 · Client Case · Pan-Asian midscale hotel group · Hospitality Real client case

A regional hotel group spanning various APAC destinations was held back by its own website: messy URLs, pages rendered in the browser, and no pages for how travellers actually search.

01
What the site had

Old URLs that had grown messy over time, pages rendered in the visitor’s browser, and page titles that did not follow how travellers search. Crawlers that do not run JavaScript, which includes many AI crawlers, saw little of the site.

02
What we found

Travellers search for a destination, things to do and the deals on offer before they choose a hotel. The site’s travel content sat on old URLs outside a clear structure, and it had no country, city or promotion pages built for those searches.

03
The real gap

Keeping rankings through the move would not be enough. The new site had to be readable by AI crawlers from day one and give AI assistants destination answers worth citing.

07 · Client Case · Pan-Asian midscale hotel group · What we did

We rebuilt the site in one migration: new structure, new page templates, a new content system and new destination content, and kept its traffic through the move.

01
Moved the site onto a new structure

Every old URL mapped to its place in the new structure with permanent redirects; XML sitemaps rebuilt. Rendering moved from the browser to the server, so search and AI crawlers read complete HTML. Hotel structured data on country, city and hotel pages. Page titles regrouped by search intent.

02
Redesigned every page type

New layouts for the hotel pages and for the destination, promotion and travel content pages around them, on one information architecture.

03
Rebuilt the content system

The site’s internal content management system was rebuilt inside the same migration, so the team can publish on the new structure without developers.

04
Built content for travel searches and AI answers

Country and city pages, promotion pages built around bank card offers, and a “hidden gems” travel series about things to do near each hotel. Live and growing since the migration.

08 · Hmlet · Executive summary

Hmlet’s inventory is live. A broken site structure confines it to branded queries and blocks organic growth.

01
Wrong language and market targeting

Because of the language and region settings on hmlet.com, the Singapore source market is served the Japanese domain: Tokyo listings, Tokyo pricing, a Mitsubishi Estate Japan footer. Neither a renter nor an AI crawler can navigate from hmlet.com to the right Singapore page; no internal link path reaches the six live Singapore property subdomains.

02
Zero non-brand visibility in search and AI answers

On the category query “best coliving in Singapore”, ChatGPT and Google’s AI Overview cite Coliwoo and Cove. Hmlet is absent from the top 20 organic results and from the cited sources, while its property pages rank only on branded queries. The inventory is indexed; it carries no non-brand demand.

03
No hreflang, no Traditional Chinese, no city navigation

On 21 October 2026 the site ran Japanese and English only, with no Traditional Chinese version or hreflang for Hong Kong, and the header navigation linked to none of its five city listing pages: Tokyo, Osaka, Fukuoka, Singapore, Hong Kong.

Search Query Analysis: Wide Gap vs Direct Rivals

Content-Mix vs Brand-Identity Mismatch High

Hmlet's single largest Singapore traffic driver is "owen house by habyt previously hmlet" (6,600 monthly searches, the highest estimated traffic value of any keyword on the domain), followed by "habyt novena previously hotel balestier" (2,900 searches). Both are searches about Hmlet's own brand-name transition -- Habyt to Hmlet -- not about coliving as a service.

Competitor Keyword Moat High

hmlet.com shares only 24 ranked keywords with coliwoo.com and 25 with cove.sg in the Singapore market -- against Coliwoo's 1,281 total ranked keywords and Cove's 2,630. Where the sets overlap, Hmlet trails on almost every term: "dormitory in singapore for rent" (Hmlet #50 vs. Coliwoo #19), "short term rent room" (Hmlet #48 vs. Cove #2), "communal living near me" (Hmlet #32 vs. Coliwoo #1). The overlap itself is thin, and Hmlet loses most of the keywords it does share.

Bright Spots: Property Names Still Rank Bright Spot

Despite the overall gap, three of Hmlet's own property names outrank Cove on shared keywords: "habyt novena" (Hmlet #3 vs. Cove #54), "habyt studios at novena" (Hmlet #2 vs. Cove #45), and "habyt studios kallang" (Hmlet #2 vs. Cove #59). Volumes are modest (110-590 monthly searches), but they show the individual property content itself ranks well when it has a clean, singular keyword to answer. Property-name queries rank well, but most buyer queries are not property names. The SEO/GEO work is to capture the far larger set of destination, stay and accommodation queries, the ones Coliwoo and Cove hold today.
