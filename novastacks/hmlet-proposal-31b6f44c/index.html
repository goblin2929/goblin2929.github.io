07 · Client Case · Pan-Asian midscale hotel group · Hospitality Real client case

A regional hotel group spanning various APAC destinations was held back by its own website: messy URLs, pages rendered in the browser, and no pages for how travellers actually search.

01
What the site had

Old URLs that had grown messy over time, pages rendered in the visitor’s browser, and page titles that did not follow how travellers search. Crawlers that do not run JavaScript, which includes many AI crawlers, saw little of the site.

02
What we found

Travellers search for a destination, things to do and the deals on offer before they choose a hotel. The site’s travel content sat on old URLs outside a clear structure, and it had no country, city or promotion pages built for those searches.

03
The real gap

Keeping rankings through the move would not be enough. The new site had to be readable by AI crawlers from day one and give AI assistants destination answers worth citing.

CLIENT CASE · HOSPITALITY · THE CHALLENGE16 / 48
07 · Client Case · Pan-Asian midscale hotel group · What we did

We rebuilt the site in one migration: new structure, new page templates, a new content system and new destination content, and kept its traffic through the move.

01
Moved the site onto a new structure

Every old URL mapped to its place in the new structure with permanent redirects; XML sitemaps rebuilt. Rendering moved from the browser to the server, so search and AI crawlers read complete HTML. Hotel structured data on country, city and hotel pages. Page titles regrouped by search intent.

02
Redesigned every page type

New layouts for the hotel pages and for the destination, promotion and travel content pages around them, on one information architecture.

03
Rebuilt the content system

The site’s internal content management system was rebuilt inside the same migration, so the team can publish on the new structure without developers.

04
Built content for travel searches and AI answers

Country and city pages, promotion pages built around bank card offers, and a “hidden gems” travel series about things to do near each hotel. Live and growing since the migration.

08 · Hmlet · Executive summary

Hmlet’s Singapore properties are live and rank by name, but the front door points to Tokyo and AI answers “best coliving in Singapore” without Hmlet.

01
The front door points the wrong way

hmlet.com now serves Hmlet Japan: Tokyo listings, Tokyo pricing, a Mitsubishi Estate Japan footer. A Singapore or Hong Kong renter who types the brand, or asks AI about Hmlet, lands on a Tokyo pitch with no path to any of the six live Singapore properties.

02
AI answers the category without Hmlet

Ask any AI system for the best coliving in Singapore and the answer names Coliwoo and Cove. Hmlet sits outside the sources AI cites for coliving here, while its property pages still rank when a buyer already knows the name. The business exists; it is not connected to the door buyers walk through.

03
The site cannot be navigated the way buyers and crawlers need

On 21 October 2026 the site ran in Japanese and English only, with no Chinese version for Hong Kong renters, and its header linked to none of its five city listings: Tokyo, Osaka, Fukuoka, Singapore, Hong Kong.

Hmlet's Biggest Keyword Is About Its Own Rebrand, Not About Coliving

Content-Mix vs Brand-Identity Mismatch High

Hmlet's single largest Singapore traffic driver is "owen house by habyt previously hmlet" (6,600 monthly searches, the highest estimated traffic value of any keyword on the domain), followed by "habyt novena previously hotel balestier" (2,900 searches). Both are searches about Hmlet's own brand-name transition -- Habyt to Hmlet -- not about coliving as a service. Meanwhile the page a Singapore search would actually land on, hmlet.com/en, presents as a Japan corporate rental brand. The traffic that exists is driven by brand-name confusion, not by category demand the way it is for both rivals.

Competitor Keyword Moat High

hmlet.com shares only 24 ranked keywords with coliwoo.com and 25 with cove.sg in the Singapore market -- against Coliwoo's 1,281 total ranked keywords and Cove's 2,630. Where the sets overlap, Hmlet trails on almost every term: "dormitory in singapore for rent" (Hmlet #50 vs. Coliwoo #19), "short term rent room" (Hmlet #48 vs. Cove #2), "communal living near me" (Hmlet #32 vs. Coliwoo #1). The overlap itself is thin, and Hmlet loses most of the keywords it does share.

Bright Spots: Property Names Still Rank Bright Spot

Despite the overall gap, three of Hmlet's own property names outrank Cove on shared keywords: "habyt novena" (Hmlet #3 vs. Cove #54), "habyt studios at novena" (Hmlet #2 vs. Cove #45), and "habyt studios kallang" (Hmlet #2 vs. Cove #59). Volumes are modest (110-590 monthly searches), but they show the individual property content itself ranks well when it has a clean, singular keyword to answer -- the gap is reach and architecture, not the underlying property pages.

Cove Already Competes in Japan High

Cove's header links to sister sites in Japan (covejapan.com), Korea (covekorea.com) and Indonesia (cove.id). covejapan.com is a Japanese language rental site for homes in Japan, with English support, no guarantor and no key money: the same offer Hmlet makes on hmlet.com. For renters in Singapore and Hong Kong looking for a home in Japan, Cove is a direct competitor, and it already runs separate sites per market.
